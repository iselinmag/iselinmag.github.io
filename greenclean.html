GreenClean

A mobile app that helps people wash clothes in a more eco-friendly way. It explains microplastics, compares detergents on environment and price, and scans products for quick info.

  • Team 4 students
  • Course Introduction to Design of Digital Products, Østfold University College
  • When Autumn 2024

The problem

We started from UN Sustainable Development Goal 6.3: better water quality through less pollution. Microplastics from clothes end up in the ocean every time we do the laundry.

  • 80%+ of respondents did not know clothes contain microplastics
  • 18 questions in our survey about laundry habits
  • 8 people in our usability test

The survey showed that the real problem is a lack of accessible information. Most people choose detergent by price, smell and how well it works, and few want to change routines unless it is easy and saves money.

How we worked

  1. Insight Survey on habits and knowledge
  2. Personas Two users based on the answers
  3. Storyboard Anna's journey with the problem
  4. Prototype Brainstorming, wireframes and Figma
  5. Test Usability test with think-aloud

Who we designed for

The prototype

From brainstorming and brainwriting we kept three core ideas, then built wireframes, a flow diagram and an interactive prototype in Figma. The logo and several icons were drawn by the team.

  • Scan products

    Point the camera at a barcode to see the product's environmental impact.

  • Compare detergents

    Pick a brand and compare price and how eco-friendly it is.

  • Microplastics in clothes

    Choose a material to learn how much it sheds in the wash.

What testing showed

We tested with 8 people, from young students to older adults, using the think-aloud method.

Feedback

  • Users praised how simple the app was
  • Some got lost because they expected "Contact us" under Information
  • Buttons felt too rounded and "bubbly", colours too pale
  • Footer icons were too big, labels hard to read
  • Older users liked the price info most; younger users cared more about money than the environment

Changes we made

  • Clearer navigation
  • Less rounded, more readable buttons
  • Smaller icons in the footer
  • New typeface under the labels
  • A more modern, professional colour palette

What I learned

  • Start with real data: the survey changed what we thought the problem was
  • Personas and a storyboard keep the team designing for real people
  • Details like button shape and colour decide whether an app feels trustworthy
  • An eco-friendly product also has to make sense for people's wallet
